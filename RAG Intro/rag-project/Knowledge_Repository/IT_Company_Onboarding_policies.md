# IT Company Onboarding Policies – All Departments

## 1. Pre-Onboarding Preparation
Before a new employee joins, the HR and IT departments must prepare all necessary resources, including system access, email accounts, and workstation setup. A checklist of required tools, software licenses, and security credentials must be completed in advance. Pre-onboarding ensures a smooth start and minimizes downtime on the employee's first day. It also helps managers focus on training rather than administrative setup.

## 2. Welcome and Orientation Session
All new hires must attend a structured orientation program. This session introduces company culture, mission, values, and policies. Department heads should provide an overview of team structure, roles, and ongoing projects. Orientation helps employees integrate faster and understand their responsibilities in context.

## 3. IT System Access and Account Creation
The IT department is responsible for provisioning all system accounts before the first working day. This includes email, internal portals, VPN, collaboration tools, and department-specific software. Password policies and multi-factor authentication must be communicated clearly. Secure system access ensures employees can start their work efficiently without security risks.

## 4. Workstation and Equipment Setup
All necessary hardware, including laptops, monitors, and peripherals, must be ready and tested. The IT team should ensure software installation, network connectivity, and VPN access are functioning. Employees must be guided on handling company-provided devices securely. Proper setup prevents technical issues and improves productivity from day one.

## 5. Department-Specific Training
Every department should provide training tailored to their operations, tools, and workflows. This includes job-specific software tutorials, documentation review, and process demonstrations. Department mentors should guide new hires during the initial weeks. Structured training ensures employees quickly adapt and contribute effectively.

## 6. HR Policies and Compliance Briefing
HR must conduct a session covering company policies, code of conduct, ethics, and compliance requirements. Topics include leave policies, attendance, workplace safety, and legal obligations. Employees should acknowledge understanding via signed documentation. This ensures alignment with corporate standards and legal requirements.

## 7. Mentorship and Buddy Assignment
Each new employee should be paired with a mentor or buddy from their department. Mentors help answer questions, provide guidance, and facilitate social integration. Regular check-ins during the first 90 days are recommended. Mentorship accelerates learning and fosters a supportive work environment.

## 8. Performance Expectations and Goal Setting
Managers must communicate role-specific performance expectations and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). Employees should collaboratively set short-term and long-term goals. Frequent feedback sessions during the probation period help track progress. Clear expectations promote accountability and productivity.

## 9. Security and Data Protection Training
All employees must undergo mandatory cybersecurity and data protection training. This covers password management, phishing awareness, and handling confidential data. IT and HR must monitor completion and understanding of security protocols. Security training reduces risks of data breaches and ensures regulatory compliance.

## 10. Feedback and Continuous Improvement
New hires should be encouraged to provide feedback about the onboarding experience. HR and department managers should review feedback to refine the onboarding process. Continuous improvement ensures future employees experience a smoother, more effective onboarding journey. This also boosts employee engagement and retention.

## 11. Employee Onboarding Checklist
New employees should follow a structured onboarding checklist provided by HR and their department. This includes completing all forms, submitting required documents, and setting up system accounts. Employees must verify their workstation setup, access to software tools, and email functionality. Following a checklist ensures nothing is missed and the onboarding process is completed smoothly and efficiently.

## 12. Communication and Support Guidelines
Employees should proactively communicate with their managers, mentors, and IT support for any questions or issues during onboarding. Use official channels such as email, internal chat, or helpdesk tickets for queries. Regular updates on progress, completed tasks, and pending items should be shared. Clear communication ensures timely problem resolution and helps employees integrate faster into the organization.
